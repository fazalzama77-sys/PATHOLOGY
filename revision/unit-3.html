1 · Definitions & structure

Willis' definition — learn it verbatim "A neoplasm is an abnormal mass of tissue, the growth of which exceeds and is uncoordinated with that of the normal tissue, and persists in the same excessive manner after cessation of the stimulus which evoked the change."
Two components of every tumour:
PARENCHYMA
the neoplastic cells — they decide the name and behaviour+ STROMA
host-derived connective tissue + blood vessels — decides the growth and consistency
  • Desmoplasia / scirrhous = abundant fibrous stroma → a stony-hard tumour
  • Medullary = little stroma → soft, fleshy tumour
  • Oncology = study of tumours · cancer = any malignant tumour

The "-oma" exceptions — MALIGNANT despite the name ★ Lymphoma · Melanoma · Seminoma · Mesothelioma · Hepatoma · Thymoma (locally invasive). Also remember the non-neoplastic "-omas": haematoma, granuloma, hamartoma.
Three look-alike terms Hamartoma — a disorganised mass of tissue native to the site. Choristoma — normal tissue in the WRONG place (ectopic). Teratoma — from germ cells, contains more than one germ layer (hair, teeth, bone in an ovarian mass).
Rule: malignant tumour of epithelium = CARCINOMA; of mesenchyme = SARCOMA. A carcinosarcoma has both.

4 · ANAPLASIA — the microscopic checklist

  • Pleomorphism — variation in cell and nuclear size and shape
  • Hyperchromasia — dark nuclei from ↑ DNA
  • High nucleo-cytoplasmic ratio — up to 1:1
  • Prominent, multiple, irregular nucleoli
  • Abnormal mitotic figures — tripolar, quadripolar, ring; ↑ mitotic index
  • Tumour giant cells with single huge or multiple nuclei
  • Loss of polarity — disorderly, chaotic arrangement
  • Ischaemic necrosis in the centre of the mass
The precancer ladder Hyperplasia → Metaplasia → DYSPLASIA (reversible, pre-neoplastic) → Carcinoma in situ (full-thickness change, basement membrane INTACT) → Invasive carcinoma (basement membrane BREACHED).

7 · Tumour immunity

  • Tumour-specific antigen (TSA) — found only on tumour cells; tumour-associated antigen (TAA) — also on some normal cells
  • Effectors: cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CD8, the main one) · NK cells (kill MHC-I-negative cells) · activated macrophages · antibody with ADCC
  • Immunosurveillance — the immune system continuously removes transformed cells; tumours appear when it fails, hence more tumours in immunosuppressed animals
  • Escape mechanisms: loss of MHC class I · antigen shedding & blocking antibody · T-reg and TGF-β mediated suppression · tumour cells outgrow the response (sneaking through) · antigenic modulation
Classic proof in veterinary science TVT regresses spontaneously in an immunocompetent dog — the standard teaching example of effective tumour immunity.

13 · Hb, PCV, TEC and the three INDICES ★★★

  • Haemoglobin — cyanmethaemoglobin (HiCN) method with Drabkin's solution is the international REFERENCE method, read at 540 nm. Sahli's acid-haematin is the visual, less accurate field method. Oxyhaemoglobin method is also used.
  • PCV / haematocrit — microhaematocrit capillary spun at high speed for ~5 min (the routine method), or the Wintrobe tube. The buffy coat between plasma and red cells holds the leucocytes and platelets.
  • TEC and TLC — Neubauer counting chamber. RBC diluting fluid = Hayem's; WBC diluting fluid = Turk's (glacial acetic acid lyses the red cells, gentian violet stains the leucocyte nuclei).
IndexFormulaUnitTells you
MCVPCV (%) × 10 ÷ RBC (millions/µl)flCell SIZE — macro / normo / microcytic
MCHHb (g/dl) × 10 ÷ RBC (millions/µl)pgHb weight per cell

Which index needs which value ★ MCV needs PCV and RBC. MCH needs Hb and RBC. MCHC needs Hb and PCV — it is the only index that does not use the red cell count, so it is the most reliable when the counter is unreliable. Hypochromia is a low MCHC.
ESR Westergren (200 mm tube, citrate 1:4) and Wintrobe (100 mm tube) methods. ESR is fastest in the HORSE (marked rouleaux) and very slow in cattle, sheep and goats. It rises with ↑ fibrinogen and globulin (inflammation, pregnancy) and falls with polycythaemia and dehydration.

17 · BIOPSY & CYTOLOGY

  • Biopsy types: excisional (whole lesion out — diagnosis + treatment) · incisional/wedge · needle-core (Tru-cut) · punch · endoscopic · bone-marrow aspirate
  • Cytology techniques: fine-needle aspiration · impression (touch) smear · scraping · swab · EXFOLIATIVE cytology of fluids — pleural, peritoneal, CSF, tracheal wash, vaginal, urine
  • Fixation: air-dry for Romanowsky stains; wet-fix immediately in 95% ethanol for Papanicolaou and H&E
  • Cytological criteria of malignancy — anisocytosis and anisokaryosis, high N:C ratio, multiple prominent angular nucleoli, coarse chromatin, abnormal mitoses, multinucleation, cannibalism
Cytology vs histopathology Cytology is rapid, cheap, minimally invasive, but it cannot show tissue ARCHITECTURE and therefore cannot prove INVASION. Histopathology remains the gold standard for the final diagnosis.

18 · NECROPSY — objectives & procedure ★★

Objectives — establish the cause of death · confirm or correct the clinical diagnosis · detect disease in the rest of the herd (surveillance) · insurance and legal requirements · check response to treatment · teaching and research.
Pre-necropsy rules — obtain full history and written owner consent (in a veterolegal case, a police or magistrate requisition replaces it) · perform as soon as possible after death, in daylight · wear protective clothing, remember zoonoses (anthrax, rabies, brucellosis, TB, glanders, leptospirosis) · arrange disinfectant and safe carcass disposal.
THE ANTHRAX RULE ★★★ Never open a ruminant found suddenly dead with unclotted tarry blood at the orifices. Take a peripheral blood smear first and stain with polychrome methylene blue (McFadyean's reaction). Only if anthrax is excluded may the carcass be opened. Dispose by deep burial with quicklime or incineration.
ItemStandard practice
PositionRuminants and horses — LEFT lateral recumbency (left side down) so the rumen lies down and the right-side viscera are exposed. Dog, cat, pig, poultry — DORSAL recumbency
TechniquesVirchow — organs removed one by one · Rokitansky — dissected in situ · Ghon — removed en bloc by systems · Letulle — all viscera removed en masse
Wild animalsChemical immobilisation records, photograph everything, check for poaching wounds, collect samples for toxicology, follow forest-department protocol

21 · PM REPORT · VETEROLEGAL NECROPSY & WOUNDS ★★

The post-mortem report — three parts
  • Preliminary — identification of the animal (species, breed, age, sex, marks, tag), owner, history, date, time and place of death and of the necropsy, condition of the carcass
  • Gross findings — organ by organ, DESCRIBE only; state size, shape, colour, consistency, distribution. Do not write a diagnosis in this section
  • Conclusion — morphological diagnosis, aetiological diagnosis, cause of death, samples sent, remarks, signature with registration number
Veterolegal necropsy — the rules Carried out only on a written requisition from the police or a magistrate · establish the identity of the animal beyond doubt · photograph everything · perform in the presence of the investigating officer · samples are sealed with the officer's seal and the chain of custody is documented · write in ink, no overwriting or erasure, correct by scoring through and initialling · the report goes to the requisitioning authority, not the owner · retain a copy.
WoundDistinguishing feature
IncisedSharp edge; LENGTH greater than DEPTH; clean margins, bleeds freely
Stab / puncturedDEPTH greater than LENGTH; small external opening
LaceratedBlunt force; irregular torn ragged margins, tissue bridges, hair driven in
Contused (bruise)Blunt force with skin intact; colour changes with age of the bruise
AbrasionSuperficial, loss of epidermis only
FirearmEntry — small, inverted, with a collar of abrasion and possibly scorching/tattooing. Exit — larger, everted, no scorching
ANTEMORTEM vs POSTMORTEM WOUND ★★★ An antemortem wound shows a VITAL REACTION — free arterial haemorrhage with clotting, infiltration of blood into the surrounding tissue, gaping and retraction of edges, inflammation, and later signs of healing. A postmortem wound shows none of these — the edges do not gape, blood merely oozes and does not clot into the tissue.
