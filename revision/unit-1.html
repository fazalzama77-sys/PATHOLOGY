Names always asked Virchow — father of modern/cellular pathology; added functio laesa. Celsus — first 4 cardinal signs. Morgagni — organ pathology. Bichat — tissue. Hippocrates — humoral.

2 · Causes of disease

INTRINSIC — genetic (lethal/sub-lethal genes, freemartin 60,XX/XY chimera); immunological; age · sex · species · breed.
EXTRINSIC — physical (trauma, heat, cold, radiation, electricity, pressure); chemical (poisons, drugs, metals); nutritional (deficiency, excess, imbalance); biological (virus, bacteria, fungi, protozoa, helminth, arthropod, prion).
One-liner A predisposing factor makes disease likely; the determining/specific cause produces it.

3 · Branches & course of disease

Branches — General (basic processes) · Systemic/Special (organ-wise) · Clinical (blood, urine, cytology) · Surgical (biopsy) · Experimental · Comparative · Forensic/veterolegal.
CourseMeaningCourseMeaning
PeracuteHours, often found deadSubacuteDays–weeks

Incubation = infection → first sign · Prodromal = vague early signs · Syndrome = group of signs occurring together · Lesion = structural change · Symptom (felt) vs Sign (observed — all veterinary findings are signs).
Diagnosis ladder Morphological dx (e.g. "acute diffuse fibrinous pneumonia") → Aetiological dx (e.g. "pasteurellosis") → Disease dx ("haemorrhagic septicaemia").

2c · THE MASTER CHART — cell under stress

NORMAL CELL — steady state
↓ increased demand / mild persistent stress
ADAPTATION — hypertrophy · hyperplasia · atrophy · metaplasia (reversible, the cell is still alive and working)
↓ stress exceeds adaptive capacity
REVERSIBLE INJURY — cloudy swelling, hydropic and fatty change (remove the cause and the cell recovers)
↓ severe or persistent: membrane + mitochondrial failure
IRREVERSIBLE INJURY → CELL DEATH
↓
NECROSIS (groups of cells, inflammation)  ·  APOPTOSIS (single cells, no inflammation)
Six causes of cell injury Hypoxia / ischaemia (the commonest) · physical agents · chemicals and drugs · infectious agents · immunological reactions · genetic and nutritional derangements.
Hypoxia = low oxygen but blood is still flowing. Ischaemia = loss of blood supply, so oxygen and substrate are cut off and waste accumulates — therefore ischaemia injures faster than hypoxia.

4b · Why an animal bleeds · what follows

Haemorrhagic diathesis — 4 groups
GroupExamples
Vessel wallVitamin C deficiency, endotoxaemia, anthrax, classical swine fever, viral vasculitis, purpura haemorrhagica
PlateletsThrombocytopenia — ehrlichiosis, bracken fern, immune-mediated, DIC
Clotting factorsSweet clover (dicoumarol), warfarin rodenticide, vitamin K deficiency, liver failure, haemophilia A (factor VIII)
CombinationDIC, snake envenomation, aflatoxicosis
Effects depend on rate, volume and site. Rapid loss of more than one-third of blood volume is fatal; the same loss slowly is tolerated. A small bleed into the brain stem or pericardium kills, a large one into muscle does not.
Fate of shed blood — RBC lysis → haemoglobin → haemosiderin (iron+, Perls blue) and haematoidin (iron-free, golden). Colour march of a bruise: red → blue → green → yellow-brown.
PM artefacts that mimic haemorrhage Hypostatic congestion — blood settles in the down side, uniform, gravity-dependent, no swelling. Haemoglobin imbibition — red staining of intima and endocardium after death. Neither is an antemortem lesion.

5b · Oedema — what you actually see

  • Gross: tissue swollen and doughy, pits on pressure; cut surface wet, glistening, drips clear straw fluid; subcutis gelatinous
  • Micro: pale eosinophilic homogeneous fluid separating fibres, widened interstitium, dilated lymphatics
  • Pulmonary oedema — heavy wet lungs, froth in trachea and bronchi, alveoli filled with pink fluid; the animal drowns in its own plasma
  • Cerebral oedema — flattened gyri, narrowed sulci, coning of the cerebellum into the foramen magnum
The distinction that answers most questions Inflammatory oedema = EXUDATE (high protein, cells, clots, Rivalta +). Non-inflammatory oedema = TRANSUDATE (low protein, no clot, Rivalta −).

9 · DIC — "consumption coagulopathy"

Trigger: endotoxin, sepsis, heat stroke, snake bite, neoplasia, severe trauma→ Widespread microthrombi→ Consumption of platelets & clotting factors→ Paradoxical BLEEDING
Lab: ↓platelets · ↑PT & APTT · ↓fibrinogen · ↑FDPs / D-dimer · schistocytes (fragmented RBCs) on smear. PM: petechiae/ecchymoses everywhere + fibrin microthrombi in glomeruli, lung, liver.

12 · Embolism

Thromboembolism ~99% · fat (long-bone fracture) · air/gas (IV injection, bends) · septic (valvular endocarditis → embolic nephritis) · parasitic (Strongylus vulgaris — cranial mesenteric a. of horse) · neoplastic · amniotic fluid · foreign body.
Direction rule Venous emboli lodge in the LUNG. Arterial/left-heart emboli go to brain, kidney, spleen, limbs. Paradoxical embolus crosses a septal defect. Saddle thrombus at the aortic trifurcation = classic cat with HCM.

13 · Infarct — RED vs WHITE

Red (haemorrhagic)White (pale/anaemic)
TissueLoose, spongySolid, compact

OcclusionOften venousArterial

Shape = wedge/cone, apex toward the occluded vessel, base at capsule. Necrosis = coagulative, except the BRAIN which liquefies. Old infarct = pale depressed scar.

10b · What decides whether an infarct forms

  • Type of blood supply — a dual supply (lung, liver) protects; an end-artery (kidney, spleen, retina) does not
  • Rate of occlusion — slow occlusion lets collaterals open, so no infarct forms
  • Vulnerability to hypoxia — neuron 3–5 min < myocardium 20–30 min < fibroblast hours
  • Oxygen content of the blood — anaemia and heart failure make infarction more likely
Timeline of a white infarct 0–24 h pale, swollen, with a red hyperaemic rim → 2–4 days yellow-white, sharply demarcated, leucocytes at the margin → 1–2 weeks granulation tissue moves in → weeks to months a depressed pale fibrous scar.

14 · Reversible cell injury

Hypoxia→↓ATP→ Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase fails→ Na⁺ + H₂O enter→Cell swelling
  • Cloudy swelling — earliest & commonest; organ pale, swollen, heavy, dull "parboiled", cut surface bulges
  • Hydropic/vacuolar — larger clear vacuoles
  • Hyaline — glassy pink homogeneous (Russell bodies, hyaline casts)
  • Zenker's (waxy) — of skeletal muscle: white muscle disease, blackleg, capture myopathy
  • Mucoid/mucinous degeneration
Point of no return Membrane damage + mitochondrial permeability transition + massive Ca²⁺ influx. Clinically = leaked enzymes (ALT, AST, CK, LDH).

15 · FATTY CHANGE & GLYCOGEN ★

FATTY CHANGE (steatosis) — abnormal triglyceride accumulation, chiefly in the LIVER (also heart, kidney).
↑ FFA delivery·↑ TG synthesis· ↓ Apoprotein / lipoprotein export·↓ β-oxidation
  • Causes: hypoxia · ketosis of crossbred cows · pregnancy toxaemia of ewes/does · feline hepatic lipidosis · aflatoxin · CCl₄, phosphorus · protein-energy malnutrition · diabetes mellitus · copper & choline deficiency
  • Gross: liver enlarged, yellow, greasy, soft, rounded borders, greasy knife; cut surface bulges; FLOATS in water/formalin
  • Micro (H&E): sharply punched-out clear round vacuoles displacing the nucleus — the fat is dissolved out by paraffin processing
  • To prove it is fat: FROZEN section + Sudan III / IV (orange-red), Oil Red O (red), Osmium tetroxide (black)
Fatty change vs fatty infiltration Fatty change = fat inside parenchymal cells (pathological). Fatty infiltration = mature adipocytes between cells (obesity, largely physiological).
Glycogen Excess in diabetes mellitus, hyperadrenocorticism/steroids ("steroid hepatopathy" of the dog), glycogen storage disease. Stain PAS → magenta; confirm by diastase digestion — glycogen disappears, so PAS+ / diastase-labile. Fix in alcohol or Carnoy's, because aqueous fixatives dissolve glycogen.

23 · AMYLOIDOSIS

  • β-pleated sheet, non-branching fibrils 7.5–10 nm + P component
  • AA (secondary/reactive) from SAA — commonest in animals; chronic suppuration, TB, hyperimmunised horses. AL (primary) = Ig light chain
  • Order: KIDNEY (glomerulus) first → liver → spleen → adrenal. Renal amyloid → protein-losing nephropathy
  • Sago spleen = nodular/follicular · Lardaceous (bacon) spleen = diffuse red pulp
Stains ★★★ Congo red → salmon-red in ordinary light, APPLE-GREEN BIREFRINGENCE in polarised light. Methyl/crystal violet → metachromatic pink-red. Thioflavin T → fluorescent. Gross: Lugol's iodine + dilute H₂SO₄ → mahogany brown → blue-violet.

Beneficial — dilution of toxin, delivery of antibody and drugs, fibrin traps organisms, phagocytosis, immunity, framework for repair.

Type III → Type I, weeks–months
  • Granulation tissue = new capillaries + fibroblasts + loose ECM + inflammatory cells. Red, granular, bleeds easily. NOT the same as granuloma.
  • Tensile strength ~10% at 1 week → maximum only 70–80% by 3 months. Never 100%.
  • Vitamin C — hydroxylation of proline & lysine · Zinc — epithelialisation · Copper — lysyl oxidase

Complications & cell types Dehiscence · keloid · exuberant granulation tissue = "PROUD FLESH", classic on the distal limb of the HORSE · contracture · adhesions. Delayed by infection (most important), foreign body, movement, poor blood supply, corticosteroids, protein/vit-C/zinc deficiency.
Labile (skin, gut, marrow) always divide · Stable (liver, kidney, pancreas) divide on demand · Permanent (neuron, cardiac & skeletal muscle) heal by SCAR only.

26b · Why autoimmunity happens

  • Release of sequestered antigen — lens, sperm, thyroglobulin, CNS myelin never met the immune system in fetal life
  • Molecular mimicry — a microbial antigen resembles a host antigen
  • Alteration of self antigen by drug, virus or hapten
  • Loss of regulatory T-cell control or polyclonal lymphocyte activation
  • Genetic predisposition — MHC haplotype and breed
Tests to remember Coombs test — antibody on red cells, for AIHA and neonatal isoerythrolysis. ANA — for SLE. LE cell — a neutrophil that has engulfed a nucleus. Direct immunofluorescence of skin biopsy — for pemphigus. Tuberculin / johnin — Type IV delayed reaction.
