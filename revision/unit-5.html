1 · WHY BIRDS ARE DIFFERENT ★★★

The single most important avian fact The bird's granulocyte is the HETEROPHIL, not the neutrophil. It has rod- or spindle-shaped eosinophilic granules and, critically, it LACKS MYELOPEROXIDASE and lysozyme. Therefore avian purulent inflammation cannot liquefy the debris and forms a DRY, FIRM, CASEOUS (inspissated) mass instead of flowing pus. This is why the "abscess" of a bird is always a caseous nodule or plug — in the trachea, air sac, or joint.

2 · RANIKHET DISEASE (Newcastle disease) ★★★

  • Avian paramyxovirus-1 (APMV-1), Paramyxoviridae. Notifiable. The most important poultry disease of India
  • Pathotypes by virulence: LENTOGENIC (mild — F1, B1, LaSota vaccine strains) · MESOGENIC (moderate — R2B "Mukteswar", Komarov, used as boosters) · VELOGENIC (severe — viscerotropic VVND and neurotropic)
The three lesions that name it ★ ① HAEMORRHAGE / NECROTIC ULCERS ON THE TIPS OF THE PROVENTRICULAR GLANDS — pinpoint red dots in a row at the base of the papillae
② NECROSIS AND ULCERATION OF THE CAECAL TONSILS AND PEYER'S PATCHES — raised, "boat-shaped" or button-like ulcers along the intestine
③ HAEMORRHAGIC TRACHEITIS and congestion of the whole respiratory tract
Also: greenish watery diarrhoea, gasping, NERVOUS SIGNS — torticollis (twisted neck), paralysis of legs and wings, opisthotonus, sudden drop in egg production with soft-shelled and depigmented eggs, ovarian follicle haemorrhage and rupture (yolk peritonitis).
Micro: non-suppurative encephalomyelitis — perivascular cuffing, neuronal degeneration, gliosis. Diagnosis: HA/HI test, virus isolation in embryonated eggs, RT-PCR.

3 · IBD (Gumboro) & CHICKEN INFECTIOUS ANAEMIA ★★

DiseaseAgent & lesions
INFECTIOUS BURSAL DISEASE
(Gumboro)		Birnavirus (double-stranded RNA, two segments); extremely resistant in the environment. Affects chicks of about 3–6 weeks, when the bursa is at its largest.
Target = BURSA OF FABRICIUS: first swollen to 2–3× normal, oedematous, with a yellow gelatinous transudate on the serosa, often haemorrhagic ("strawberry bursa"); then by about day 8 it ATROPHIES and becomes small and grey.
"PAINT-BRUSH" HAEMORRHAGES in the thigh and pectoral muscles are the classic second lesion; also nephrosis with urate, and a dehydrated darkened carcass.
The real damage is IMMUNOSUPPRESSION — B-cell destruction means vaccination failures against Ranikhet and other diseases
CHICKEN INFECTIOUS ANAEMIAGyrovirus, family Circoviridae; vertically transmitted; young chicks about 2–4 weeks. Destroys haemocytoblasts in the bone marrow and T-cell precursors in the thymus.
APLASTIC ANAEMIA — pale, fatty, yellow-white bone marrow (normally red), PCV falls below about 27%, pale carcass, THYMIC ATROPHY, haemorrhages in muscle and subcutis. With secondary Clostridium it produces "BLUE WING DISEASE" / gangrenous dermatitis of the wing tips. Strongly immunosuppressive

5 · FOWL POX & AVIAN INFLUENZA

DiseaseKey facts
FOWL POXAvipoxvirus; spread by MOSQUITOES and through skin abrasions — hence seasonal.
CUTANEOUS (dry) form — nodular wart-like scabs on the comb, wattles, eyelids and other unfeathered skin; usually mild, self-limiting.
DIPHTHERITIC (wet) form — yellow-white CASEOUS plaques firmly adherent to the mouth, pharynx, larynx and trachea, which peel off leaving a bleeding ulcer; this form kills by suffocation and carries much higher mortality.
Micro: epidermal hyperplasia with ballooning degeneration and large INTRACYTOPLASMIC BOLLINGER BODIES containing the elementary Borrel bodies
AVIAN INFLUENZAInfluenza A; HPAI strains such as H5N1 and H5N8 — notifiable and zoonotic. Wild waterfowl are the reservoir.
Sudden very high mortality, often before signs are noticed. Cyanosis and oedema of the comb and wattles, subcutaneous oedema of the head and neck, haemorrhages on the shanks and feet ("red hocks"), petechiae on the proventriculus, epicardium and abdominal fat, and necrotic foci in the pancreas and spleen. Severe egg drop.
Low-pathogenic strains cause only mild respiratory disease and egg drop

7 · Reticuloendotheliosis · Avian encephalomyelitis · Avian nephritis

DiseaseKey point
ReticuloendotheliosisRetrovirus (REV), unrelated to ALV. Produces a runting–stunting syndrome with abnormal feathering, plus lymphoma of liver, spleen and other viscera. Because it can also enlarge peripheral nerves, it is the one condition that genuinely mimics Marek's disease. Also a common contaminant of vaccines
AVIAN ENCEPHALOMYELITIS
("epidemic tremor")		Picornavirus, mainly VERTICALLY transmitted; affects chicks under about 3 weeks. Ataxia, sitting on hocks, progressive paresis, and fine rapid TREMORS of the HEAD and NECK — obvious when the bird is held. Adults show only a transient egg drop; survivors may develop cataract.
There are no useful gross lesions. Micro: non-suppurative encephalomyelitis with CENTRAL CHROMATOLYSIS of neurons, perivascular cuffing, and LYMPHOID NODULES IN THE MUSCULAR LAYER OF THE GIZZARD and proventriculus — this is the diagnostic histological lesion
Avian nephritisAstrovirus; young chicks. Interstitial nephritis with pale swollen kidneys, urate deposition and visceral gout, growth retardation and runting. Often subclinical but adds to other nephropathies

9 · EGG DROP SYNDROME & REOVIRUS ★

DiseaseKey facts
EGG DROP SYNDROME (EDS-76)Caused by a DUCK ADENOVIRUS (Atadenovirus) — ducks and geese are the natural host and reservoir. Vertically transmitted, the virus stays latent until point of lay.
The classic picture: a sudden drop in egg production of 10–40% in apparently HEALTHY birds with NO other signs. The eggs are SHELL-LESS, thin-shelled, soft, rough or DEPIGMENTED (loss of brown colour), with watery albumen.
Lesion: oedema and inflammation of the SHELL GLAND (pouch) of the oviduct, with intranuclear inclusion bodies in its epithelium; inactive ovaries and atrophic oviduct. Diagnosis by HI test
REOVIRUS infections① VIRAL ARTHRITIS / TENOSYNOVITIS — the important one. Swelling above the hock, inflammation of the gastrocnemius and digital flexor tendon sheaths with oedema and later fibrosis; in chronic cases the GASTROCNEMIUS TENDON RUPTURES, giving a green-black bruise above the hock and permanent lameness.
② Malabsorption / runting–stunting ("helicopter disease") — poor growth, abnormal feathering, pale, undigested feed in the droppings, brittle bones.
Also enteritis and hepatitis

10 · AVIAN COLIBACILLOSIS ★★★

THE TRIAD — the single most-asked avian gross picture Colisepticaemia produces the classic trio of fibrinous PERIHEPATITIS (a dull white fibrin film peeling off the liver capsule), fibrinous PERICARDITIS (thick opaque pericardium adherent to the heart) and fibrinous AIRSACCULITIS (air sacs thickened, cloudy, with yellow caseous sheets). Any one of these three in a chicken should make you say E. coli.
FormDescription
Omphalitis / yolk sac infection"MUSHY CHICK" — wet, swollen, foul-smelling navel in the first week; unabsorbed, discoloured, foul yolk sac; from dirty hatchery or floor eggs
ColisepticaemiaThe triad above, plus splenomegaly and congestion; usually follows respiratory damage by Ranikhet virus, IB or mycoplasma — E. coli is a secondary invader
Coligranuloma (Hjärre's disease)Firm yellow granulomas in the liver, caeca and mesentery — resembles tuberculosis but is not acid-fast
Salpingitis / egg peritonitisOviduct distended with laminated caseous casts; a common cause of death in layers
Swollen head syndrome, coliform cellulitisSubcutaneous oedema of head and periorbital tissue; yellow caseous plaques under the abdominal skin

11 · Infectious coryza · Clostridial diseases

  • INFECTIOUS CORYZA — Avibacterium (Haemophilus) paragallinarum. Marked swelling of the face and infraorbital sinus, closed swollen eyes, and a thick, sticky nasal discharge with a characteristic FOUL, OFFENSIVE ODOUR — the odour is the field diagnosis. Conjunctivitis, wattle oedema, sharp drop in egg production. Morbidity is high but mortality low; carriers persist in the flock
  • NECROTIC ENTERITIS — Clostridium perfringens type A (and C), following mucosal damage by coccidiosis or a sudden feed change. The small intestine is ballooned, thin-walled and full of dark foul gas and fluid; the mucosa is covered by a loose, brown-green, friable diphtheritic pseudomembrane described as a "TURKISH TOWEL". Sudden deaths in the best-grown birds
  • ULCERATIVE ENTERITIS ("quail disease") — Clostridium colinum; deep punched-out ulcers of intestine and caeca, with pale necrotic foci in the liver
  • GANGRENOUS DERMATITIS — Cl. septicum and Cl. perfringens entering damaged skin in birds already immunosuppressed by chicken infectious anaemia or IBD; dark, wet, crepitant, gangrenous skin of the wing tip and thigh
  • BOTULISM ("limberneck") — Cl. botulinum type C toxin, eaten in maggots or rotting carcasses; flaccid paralysis of the neck, wings and legs; no gross lesion

13 · Fowl cholera · Avian TB · Spirochaetosis

DiseaseKey facts
FOWL CHOLERAPasteurella multocida; bipolar organisms on a Leishman/Giemsa smear of heart blood or liver.
ACUTE (septicaemic): sudden death of the best birds, often found dead on the floor; cyanotic, dark purple comb and wattles; petechial and ecchymotic haemorrhages on the epicardium, serosa, abdominal fat and intestinal mucosa; the LIVER is swollen with countless PINPOINT GREY-WHITE NECROTIC FOCI; pneumonia is prominent in turkeys.
CHRONIC: localised infection — swollen oedematous WATTLES, arthritis and swollen joints, torticollis from middle-ear and cranial infection, conjunctivitis
AVIAN TUBERCULOSISMycobacterium avium; a disease of older birds, backyard flocks and zoo birds — rare in short-cycle broilers. Progressive emaciation with a prominent keel despite a good appetite, pale comb, lameness. Irregular grey-white to yellow NODULES of varying size in the LIVER, SPLEEN and INTESTINE, and often in the bone marrow; unlike mammalian TB there is little or no calcification. Ziehl–Neelsen: abundant ACID-FAST bacilli. Zoonotic risk to immunocompromised people
AVIAN SPIROCHAETOSISBorrelia anserina, transmitted by the FOWL TICK Argas persicus — so it follows tick infestation of housing. Fever, marked depression, greenish diarrhoea, progressive anaemia and paralysis. Post-mortem: greatly enlarged, mottled spleen with white foci, swollen mottled liver, pericardial effusion. Diagnosis: motile spirochaetes in a wet or Giemsa-stained blood film during the febrile phase

14 · MYCOPLASMOSIS & CHLAMYDIOSIS ★★

DiseaseKey facts
Mycoplasma gallisepticum
= CHRONIC RESPIRATORY DISEASE (CRD)		Slow-spreading, egg-transmitted, and stress-triggered. Coughing, sneezing, tracheal rales, nasal and ocular discharge, swollen infraorbital sinus ("INFECTIOUS SINUSITIS" in turkeys), poor growth and egg drop.
Lesions: catarrhal exudate in nasal passages, sinuses and trachea; thickened, cloudy air sacs with yellow caseous beads — AIRSACCULITIS. Micro: marked LYMPHOID FOLLICLE hyperplasia in the mucosa.
"COMPLICATED CRD" — when E. coli joins in, the perihepatitis–pericarditis–airsacculitis triad appears and mortality rises sharply. This is the commonest respiratory problem of Indian commercial layers
Mycoplasma synoviaeINFECTIOUS SYNOVITIS — swollen hock joints and foot pads, lameness, breast blister, pale comb; joints and tendon sheaths contain a viscous grey-yellow to creamy exudate that later becomes caseous. Also causes a subclinical airsacculitis and eggshell apex abnormalities
CHLAMYDIOSIS
(psittacosis in parrots, ornithosis in other birds)		Chlamydia psittaci, an obligate intracellular organism; notifiable ZOONOSIS that causes serious atypical pneumonia in people handling birds.
Lesions: fibrinous AIRSACCULITIS, PERICARDITIS and PERIHEPATITIS (looks like colibacillosis), with marked enlargement of the SPLEEN and LIVER and focal necrosis; conjunctivitis and greenish diarrhoea in the live bird.
Diagnosis: elementary bodies in impression smears with Giemsa, Macchiavello or Gimenez stain, plus antigen ELISA and PCR

15 · AVIAN MYCOSES

DiseaseKey facts
ASPERGILLOSIS
("brooder pneumonia")		Aspergillus fumigatus, from mouldy litter, damp feed and contaminated hatchery/setter — the spores are inhaled in enormous numbers.
Acute form in chicks — high mortality in the first two weeks, gasping without a rattle. Lesions: multiple hard, yellow-white CASEOUS NODULES (1–5 mm) scattered through the LUNGS and studding the AIR SACS; on an exposed air sac the fungus may grow visible green-grey fuzzy sporulating colonies, which is diagnostic on sight.
Micro: granulomas with septate, dichotomously branching hyphae at about 45°, giant cells; stain with PAS or Grocott's methenamine silver. Ocular and brain forms also occur. Not contagious bird-to-bird — it is an environmental disease, so the answer is always to correct the litter and feed storage
THRUSH / crop mycosis
("sour crop")		Candida albicans; follows prolonged antibiotic use, poor hygiene and vitamin A deficiency. The crop mucosa is thickened and covered with a white-grey, raised, adherent pseudomembrane whose corrugated pattern is described as a "TURKISH TOWEL"; also ulcers in the proventriculus. Birds are unthrifty with a doughy crop
FAVUS
(avian ringworm, "white comb")		Microsporum gallinae; a superficial dermatophytosis of the unfeathered skin of the comb and wattles, producing white, powdery, scaly, crusty patches that may thicken into wrinkled masses; feather loss on the head and neck follows. Chronic, disfiguring, rarely fatal

16 · AVIAN MYCOTOXICOSES ★★

ToxinTarget & lesion in the bird
AFLATOXIN
(Aspergillus flavus)		Target = LIVER. DUCKLINGS are the most susceptible of all species, followed by turkey poults; chickens are relatively more resistant. Liver is enlarged, pale to yellow, firm and fatty, with marked BILE DUCT (biliary) HYPERPLASIA in chronic cases; reduced growth and feed efficiency, pale carcass with subcutaneous and muscular haemorrhage (because clotting factors fall), poor pigmentation of shanks, drop in egg production and hatchability. Profoundly IMMUNOSUPPRESSIVE — a common hidden reason for vaccine failure and for severe IBD, IBH or coccidiosis outbreaks in India. Source: damp groundnut cake and maize
OCHRATOXIN ATarget = KIDNEY — and it is more nephrotoxic to poultry than aflatoxin. Swollen, pale, mottled kidneys with distended ureters full of white urate, and VISCERAL GOUT; polydipsia, wet litter, poor growth, poor shell quality. Also immunosuppressive
TRICHOTHECENES
(T-2 toxin, DON — Fusarium)		Direct contact necrosis wherever the toxin touches. The classic lesion is yellow-white caseous ORAL LESIONS at the COMMISSURE OF THE BEAK and on the HARD PALATE and TONGUE. Also feed refusal, gizzard erosion, intestinal necrosis, and — because trichothecenes are radiomimetic — necrosis of bone marrow and lymphoid tissue with haemorrhages, abnormal feathering and neural signs

18 · COCCIDIOSIS & HISTOMONIASIS ★★★

Eimeria speciesSite & lesion — the species is identified by WHERE it sits
E. tenellaCAECA. The most acute and most fatal, especially at 3–6 weeks. Frank BLOODY DROPPINGS; caeca are distended, thickened and full of clotted blood, which later organises into hard laminated "CAECAL CORES". High mortality
E. necatrixMID small intestine. The most damaging of the intestinal species in growers; the gut is ballooned and thickened with white spots and petechiae seen from the serosa giving a "SALT AND PEPPER" appearance
E. acervulinaDUODENUM. The commonest species. Transverse white bands or "LADDER-LIKE" white plaques across the duodenal mucosa, best seen from the serosal side. Poor growth rather than death
E. maximaMID intestine. Thickened flaccid gut with orange-pink mucus; the largest oocysts; important because it damages absorption and predisposes to necrotic enteritis
E. brunettiLOWER intestine, rectum and cloaca. Necrotic and haemorrhagic enteritis with a coagulated cast
HISTOMONIASIS — "BLACKHEAD" ★★★ Histomonas meleagridis, a flagellate protozoan. TURKEYS are highly susceptible (mortality can approach 100%); chickens are largely resistant carriers. It is carried inside the egg of the caecal worm Heterakis gallinarum, and earthworms act as transport hosts — which is why the disease persists in the soil for years.
The two lesions, both required for the answer:
① CAECA — thickened ulcerated wall containing a hard, dry, laminated, yellow-white CAECAL CORE
② LIVER — circular, depressed, yellow-grey to green necrotic foci up to 1–2 cm with a raised rim, the classic "TARGET" or "BULL'S-EYE" lesion
The name "blackhead" comes from cyanosis of the head, but this is inconstant and NOT a reliable sign — the liver lesion is.

21 · MISCELLANEOUS CONDITIONS ★★★

ConditionWhat it is
Heat strokeIndian summer, poor ventilation, high stocking. Panting, prostration, sudden death of the heaviest birds. PM: generalised congestion, pulmonary congestion and oedema, rapid onset of rigor and rapid decomposition; the carcass spoils quickly
Vent gleet (cloacitis)Chronic inflammation of the cloaca with a foul-smelling, pasty, yellow-white discharge that soils and bares the vent feathers; associated with poor hygiene, and it predisposes to vent pecking and cannibalism
INTERNAL LAYERThe ovum is ovulated but the infundibulum fails to pick it up, so the yolk drops free into the abdominal cavity → EGG-YOLK PERITONITIS: yellow, cheesy, foul yolk material plastered over the viscera, peritonitis, and death. Often follows Ranikhet disease, salpingitis or rough handling

It is caused by permanent OVIDUCT damage, classically by infectious bronchitis virus infecting the chick in its first two weeks. The ovary is active but the oviduct is blind, cystic and fluid-filled, so ovulated yolks fall into the abdomen
Pendulous cropThe crop becomes atonic, dilated and impacted, hanging as a heavy pendulous sac; sour smell; associated with fibrous feed, water deprivation followed by gorging, and Candida
Breast blister (keel bursitis)Inflammation of the sternal bursa over the keel, from wet caked litter, poor perches and any condition that makes the bird sit — leg weakness, synovitis. Starts as a clear fluid-filled swelling, becomes a hard caseous mass. A major carcass-downgrading defect
ASCITES SYNDROME
(pulmonary hypertension syndrome)		A metabolic disease of FAST-GROWING BROILERS: oxygen demand outstrips the capacity of the lung, made worse by cold, high altitude, poor ventilation and pellet feeding. Pulmonary hypertension → RIGHT VENTRICULAR HYPERTROPHY AND DILATATION → right heart failure.
PM: abdomen distended with clear straw-coloured fluid plus YELLOW GELATINOUS FIBRIN CLOTS; a dilated flabby right ventricle; and a congested, firm, "nutmeg"/cardiac LIVER often coated with fibrin. Hydropericardium may also be present
FATTY LIVER AND KIDNEY SYNDROME (FLKS)BIOTIN deficiency in fast-growing broilers of about 2–4 weeks on wheat-based low-biotin rations. Sudden death of apparently healthy chicks, often lying on the breast with the head thrown back. The LIVER and KIDNEYS are pale, swollen and fatty; hypoglycaemia is the fatal event; the heart is also pale
FATTY LIVER (haemorrhagic) SYNDROMEA quite different disease — of OVERWEIGHT CAGED LAYERS on a high-energy diet with no exercise. The liver is grossly enlarged, yellow, greasy, soft and friable, and is easily torn; large subcapsular haemorrhages and blood clots are present, and fatal rupture with massive intra-abdominal haemorrhage is the usual cause of death, together with a fall in egg production. Abundant abdominal fat
CAGE LAYER FATIGUEOsteoporosis of high-producing caged layers — calcium and phosphorus are drained for shell formation while the bird gets no exercise, so structural bone is resorbed. The hen is found lying on her side in the cage, unable to stand, but bright and eating; bones (ribs, vertebrae, femur) are soft and fracture easily, and collapse of a thoracic vertebra causes paralysis. Shells become thin
GOUT ★Deposition of URATES because birds are uricotelic and any renal failure or dehydration precipitates them.
VISCERAL gout — a chalky white "frosting" of urate crystals on the pericardium, liver capsule, air sacs and peritoneum; kidneys and ureters distended with white urate.
ARTICULAR gout — soft white "toothpaste-like" urate tophi in and around the joints and tendon sheaths of the feet and hocks, causing lameness.
Causes: nephrotoxic damage (ochratoxin, nephropathogenic IB virus, avian nephritis virus), excess dietary protein and calcium, water deprivation, sodium bicarbonate excess, vitamin A deficiency. Micro: needle-shaped urate crystals with a surrounding granulomatous "tophus" reaction
Haemorrhagic syndromeAplastic anaemia and thrombocytopenia from mycotoxins (trichothecenes, aflatoxin), sulpha drugs or chicken infectious anaemia. Pale, fatty, yellow bone marrow; haemorrhages in muscle, subcutis, and viscera; pale carcass
Round heart diseaseAn idiopathic cardiomyopathy of turkeys and broilers; the heart is globular, rounded, pale and flabby with a dilated thin-walled ventricle; sudden death
Impaction of the oviduct · egg boundA retained egg or laminated caseous mass in the oviduct or cloaca; the hen strains, and it may cause cloacal or oviduct prolapse and then vent pecking. Associated with the first large eggs of a young pullet, oversized eggs, hypocalcaemia and salpingitis
BUMBLE FOOT (pododermatitis)Infection, usually Staphylococcus aureus, entering a wound on the plantar footpad from rough perches, wire floors or wet caked litter. The foot is hot, swollen and painful, with a dark scab, and contains a firm CASEOUS CORE (remember — birds cannot make liquid pus). Leads to lameness, arthritis and osteomyelitis

22 · COMMON VICES

Cannibalism in its several forms — toe picking (chicks), feather pecking (growers), vent picking (layers, often after a prolapse — the most damaging), head picking, and egg eating.
Causes: overcrowding and high stocking density · excessively bright light · high temperature and poor ventilation · nutritional deficiency of protein, METHIONINE, salt or fibre · boredom and lack of feeder/drinker space · prolapse, vent gleet or ectoparasites drawing attention to the vent · mixing birds of unequal size.
Control: beak trimming · reduce light intensity or use red light · lower stocking density · balanced ration with adequate methionine and salt · provide roughage or pecking blocks · remove and treat injured birds at once.
