2 · FOOT AND MOUTH DISEASE ★★★

  • Aphthovirus, family Picornaviridae; ssRNA. 7 serotypes — O, A, C, SAT-1, SAT-2, SAT-3, Asia-1. In India the circulating types are O, A and Asia-1. No cross-protection between serotypes — this is why vaccination fails when the type changes
  • Cloven-footed animals. Very high morbidity (up to 100%), low mortality in adults — but high mortality in young stock
  • Gross: vesicles (aphthae) → ruptured, leaving red raw erosions, on the tongue, dental pad, gums, muzzle, interdigital space, coronary band and teats; profuse ropy salivation, lip-smacking, lameness
  • "TIGER HEART" ★ in calves — pale yellow-grey streaks of myocardial degeneration and necrosis alternating with red muscle; this is what kills the young
  • Micro: intra-epithelial vesicle from hydropic and ballooning degeneration of the stratum spinosum
  • NOTIFIABLE. Sample: vesicular fluid and epithelial flap in 50% glycerol-phosphate buffer, pH 7.2–7.6, sent chilled
The 4 vesicular diseases look IDENTICAL ★★ FMD — cattle, sheep, goat, pig; NOT horses. Vesicular stomatitis — the only one that DOES affect HORSES (and is zoonotic). Vesicular exanthema and swine vesicular disease — pigs only. They can be separated only in the laboratory. The horse is the field clue.

4 · Bluetongue · IBR · BVD-MD

DiseaseAgent & key facts
BLUETONGUEOrbivirus, Reoviridae; transmitted by Culicoides midges, therefore seasonal (rainy season) and non-contagious. SHEEP are worst affected. Facial and submandibular oedema, cyanotic swollen protruding tongue, erosions of the buccal mucosa, coronitis with a red-purple band at the coronary band and later hoof separation, torticollis. ★ Haemorrhage at the BASE OF THE PULMONARY ARTERY is the characteristic post-mortem finding. Skeletal muscle necrosis
IBR / IPVBovine herpesvirus-1. Respiratory form ("red nose") — necrotic grey-white plaques on the nasal mucosa, laryngotracheitis with a fibrinonecrotic cast. Genital form — infectious pustular vulvovaginitis / balanoposthitis. Abortion — usually in the last trimester, autolysed fetus with pinpoint necrotic foci in the liver. Also conjunctivitis and calf encephalitis. Cowdry type A intranuclear inclusions early. Establishes LATENCY in the trigeminal ganglion and recrudesces with stress
BVD / MUCOSAL DISEASE ★Pestivirus, Flaviviridae. Two biotypes: non-cytopathic (ncp) and cytopathic (cp).
The PI story — learn this: infection of the dam with the ncp biotype at about 40–120 days of gestation, before the fetal immune system matures, produces a calf that is IMMUNOTOLERANT and PERSISTENTLY INFECTED (PI). That PI animal sheds virus for life and develops fatal MUCOSAL DISEASE if it is later superinfected by the CYTOPATHIC biotype.
Lesions: linear erosions/ulcers of the oesophagus (highly suggestive), erosions from mouth to colon, Peyer's patch necrosis, interdigital erosions, lymphoid depletion. Infection at about 100–170 days instead causes CEREBELLAR HYPOPLASIA and ocular defects

5 · EQUINE viral diseases

DiseaseAgent & lesion
Equine infectious anaemia ("swamp fever")Lentivirus, Retroviridae; biting flies and contaminated needles. Recurrent fever, progressive anaemia, ventral oedema, emaciation; LIFELONG CARRIER. Pathogenesis is immune-mediated: repeated antigenic drift → immune complexes → Type III glomerulonephritis and Coombs-positive haemolysis. Micro: SIDEROLEUKOCYTES (haemosiderin-laden macrophages) in liver, spleen and bone marrow; lymphoid proliferation around vessels. Diagnosis: Coggins test (AGID) and cELISA
Equine influenzaInfluenza A, subtypes H7N7 and H3N8. Sudden dry, harsh, hacking cough, high fever, rapid spread; damage to the tracheal cilia predisposes to secondary bacterial pneumonia
Equine viral arteritisArterivirus. Panvascular disease — necrotising arteritis of small muscular arteries → oedema of limbs, ventral abdomen and periorbital tissue ("pink eye"), abortion at any stage. Stallions become long-term carriers and shed in semen
Equine rhinopneumonitisEHV-1 and EHV-4. EHV-4 → mainly upper respiratory disease in foals. EHV-1 → abortion storms in the last trimester; the aborted fetus is FRESH (not autolysed), icteric, with pleural fluid and pinpoint white necrotic foci in the liver with Cowdry type A intranuclear inclusions; also myeloencephalopathy from vasculitis and thrombosis
Equine encephalomyelitisAlphavirus (Togaviridae) — EEE, WEE, VEE; mosquito-borne, birds are the reservoir, zoonotic. Non-suppurative polioencephalomyelitis — neuronal necrosis, neuronophagia, perivascular cuffing, gliosis, chiefly in grey matter

6 · SWINE viral diseases

CLASSICAL SWINE FEVER — the four-lesion set ★★★ Pestivirus (same genus as BVD). Highly contagious, high mortality.
① "BUTTON ULCERS" (botryomycotic buttons) — raised, laminated, concentric necrotic ulcers of the caecum, colon and ileocaecal valve
② MARGINAL SPLENIC INFARCTS — dark wedge-shaped infarcts on the splenic border (near pathognomonic)
③ "TURKEY-EGG KIDNEY" — petechiae scattered over a pale renal cortex; petechiae also in bladder, larynx and skin
④ HAEMORRHAGIC ("MARBLED") LYMPH NODES — haemorrhage at the periphery of the node
Plus non-suppurative encephalitis with perivascular cuffing. Congenital infection → cerebellar hypoplasia and congenital tremor
Swine influenza — Influenza A, usually H1N1; explosive outbreak, high morbidity, low mortality; cranioventral purple-red consolidation, necrotising bronchiolitis; zoonotic potential.

7 · CANINE & FELINE viral diseases ★★

DiseaseAgent & key lesion
RABIESLyssavirus, Rhabdoviridae, bullet-shaped. Virus in saliva → centripetal spread along peripheral nerves to the CNS, then centrifugal spread to the salivary glands (that is why the bite is infective). Forms: furious, dumb/paralytic. Gross lesions are minimal or absent — foreign bodies in a dog's stomach are suggestive. Micro: non-suppurative polioencephalitis + NEGRI BODIES (intracytoplasmic, in hippocampal pyramidal neurons of carnivores and cerebellar Purkinje cells of herbivores); Babes' nodules = glial nodules.
Diagnosis: FAT on brain impression smear is the GOLD STANDARD; also Seller's stain for Negri bodies, mouse inoculation, RT-PCR. Zoonosis — 100% fatal once signs appear
CANINE DISTEMPERMorbillivirus. Pantropic — respiratory, GI, skin, CNS. Biphasic fever, oculo-nasal discharge, bronchointerstitial pneumonia, "HARD PAD" (nasodigital hyperkeratosis), enamel hypoplasia ("distemper teeth") if infected before teeth erupt, demyelinating leukoencephalitis, "chewing-gum" fits. Inclusions are BOTH intranuclear and intracytoplasmic, best seen in bladder, gastric and bronchial epithelium and astrocytes. Marked lymphoid depletion → secondary infection
INFECTIOUS CANINE HEPATITISCanine adenovirus-1. Centrilobular hepatic necrosis with Cowdry type A intranuclear inclusions in hepatocytes and endothelium; OEDEMA OF THE GALL-BLADDER WALL is characteristic; fibrin strands on the liver capsule; petechiae; "BLUE EYE" = corneal oedema and anterior uveitis, a Type III immune-complex reaction during recovery or after live vaccine
CANINE PARVOVIRUS (CPV-2)Attacks rapidly dividing cells. Intestinal CRYPT NECROSIS (not the villus tip) → villus collapse, haemorrhagic enteritis with foul bloody diarrhoea and vomiting; lymphoid depletion and marked leucopenia; myocarditis in pups infected under about 8 weeks. Intranuclear inclusions
FELINE PANLEUKOPENIAFeline parvovirus. Severe panleukopenia, crypt necrosis and enteritis, and CEREBELLAR HYPOPLASIA in kittens infected in utero or just after birth

8 · Ovine retroviral lung disease · Rota & Corona · PRIONS

DiseaseKey point
MAEDI (–visna)Ovine lentivirus. Maedi = chronic progressive INTERSTITIAL pneumonia — lungs heavy, grey-blue, rubbery, DO NOT COLLAPSE, with rib impressions and lymphoid follicle formation. Visna = demyelinating meningoencephalitis. Long incubation, no fever, no cure
JAAGSIEKTE ★Ovine pulmonary adenocarcinoma, a betaretrovirus — a genuinely CONTAGIOUS LUNG TUMOUR. Firm grey-white nodules, later confluent; abundant fluid. "WHEELBARROW TEST" — lifting the hindquarters makes copious frothy fluid pour from the nostrils
RotavirusNeonatal diarrhoea. Infects MATURE enterocytes at the VILLUS TIP → villous atrophy and blunting → malabsorptive, osmotic diarrhoea. Crypts are spared, so recovery is possible
CoronavirusTGE of pigs — very severe villous atrophy, near-100% mortality under 2 weeks. Bovine coronavirus — calf diarrhoea and winter dysentery in adults. Also feline coronavirus → FIP with pyogranulomatous vasculitis
PRION DISEASES ★The agent is PrPSc — an abnormally folded PROTEIN with NO nucleic acid. Therefore: no inflammation, no immune response, no antibody, no fever, and extreme resistance to formalin, heat, UV and ordinary disinfectants. Very long incubation, invariably fatal.
Common lesion: STATUS SPONGIOSUS — vacuolation of neurons and neuropil in the grey matter, gliosis, neuronal loss, amyloid plaques.
SCRAPIE (sheep, goats) — intense pruritus, wool loss, ataxia. BSE (cattle, "mad cow") — spread by feeding meat-and-bone meal; apprehension, hyperaesthesia, ataxia; linked to variant CJD in people. FSE in cats; chronic wasting disease in deer.
Diagnosis: histopathology of the brain stem (obex) + IMMUNOHISTOCHEMISTRY or Western blot for PrP

9 · POX VIRUS DISEASES

Macule→Papule→ Vesicle→Pustule→Crust / scab→Scar
  • Poxviruses are large DNA viruses that replicate in the CYTOPLASM, hence intracytoplasmic inclusions; micro shows ballooning degeneration of keratinocytes and epidermal hyperplasia
  • SHEEP POX — the most severe pox of domestic animals (Capripoxvirus); generalised skin nodules, and hard grey nodules in the LUNG are characteristic; "sheep-pox cells" with vacuolated nuclei
  • GOAT POX — similar, milder; LUMPY SKIN DISEASE (capripox, cattle) — multiple firm intradermal nodules over the whole body, "sit-fast" cores, lymphadenopathy, oedema of the limbs
  • Cowpox / BUFFALOPOX — teat and udder lesions, zoonotic, important in Indian dairies (milkers' nodules)
  • ORF (contagious ecthyma / contagious pustular dermatitis) — a PARAPOXVIRUS of sheep and goats; proliferative, scabby, wart-like lesions on the lips, commissures and muzzle of lambs; zoonotic; interferes with suckling
  • Swine pox — usually spread by the pig louse Haematopinus suis; Fowl pox — cutaneous (comb, wattle) and diphtheritic forms, Bollinger bodies

11 · ANTHRAX & the CLOSTRIDIA ★★★

ANTHRAX Bacillus anthracis — Gram-positive, capsulated, spore-forming rod; spores survive in soil for decades. Peracute death in ruminants. Dark tarry blood that fails to clot oozes from the nostrils, mouth, anus and vulva; rigor mortis is absent or incomplete; the SPLEEN is huge, soft and blackberry-jam-like. Widespread haemorrhage and oedema.
NEVER open the carcass. Make a peripheral blood smear and stain with polychrome methylene blue → McFADYEAN'S REACTION (blue rods with a pink capsule). Dispose by deep burial with quicklime or incineration. Notifiable, zoonotic (malignant pustule, woolsorter's disease). In pigs it is usually a localised pharyngeal/oedematous form; in horses, colic and oedema.
Clostridial diseaseOrganism & the fact that identifies it
BLACK QUARTER (blackleg)Cl. chauvoei. ENDOGENOUS — spores already in the muscle germinate after bruising, no wound needed. Young, well-nourished cattle 6 months–2 years. Heavy muscle becomes dark red-black, dry, spongy, CREPITANT with a rancid-butter odour; hot painful swelling that becomes cold and crackly. Prevented by vaccination before the rains
Malignant oedemaCl. septicum — EXOGENOUS, needs a WOUND; more oedematous, less gassy than blackleg
BLACK DISEASE
(infectious necrotic hepatitis)		Cl. novyi type B in sheep. Requires liver damage by migrating immature Fasciola to create the anaerobic focus. Large pale-yellow necrotic foci surrounded by a hyperaemic zone in the liver; after death intense subcutaneous venous congestion turns the skin dark — hence the name
ENTEROTOXAEMIACl. perfringens types. Type D = "PULPY KIDNEY" of lambs — epsilon toxin after a sudden change to rich feed; GLYCOSURIA is a useful antemortem clue; kidneys soften rapidly after death; focal symmetrical encephalomalacia. Type B = lamb dysentery; Type C = "struck" and necrotic enteritis
BRAXYCl. septicum — ABOMASITIS of sheep that eat frosted/frozen grass; haemorrhagic necrotic abomasal wall
BOTULISMCl. botulinum — an INTOXICATION: the preformed toxin is EATEN. Toxin blocks acetylcholine release at the neuromuscular junction → FLACCID paralysis, tongue paralysis, "limberneck" in birds. Type D in cattle follows PHOSPHORUS deficiency → osteophagia (chewing carcass bones)
TETANUSCl. tetani — enters through a deep puncture wound. Tetanospasmin blocks the release of the inhibitory transmitters glycine and GABA from the Renshaw cells → SPASTIC paralysis: lockjaw (trismus), "sawhorse" stance, erect ears, protrusion of the third eyelid, spasms triggered by noise or touch; the animal remains conscious. Horses are the most susceptible; there are no diagnostic gross lesions
Two toxins, opposite results BOTULISM = FLACCID (ACh release blocked). TETANUS = SPASTIC/RIGID (inhibition blocked). Both are Clostridium neurotoxins.

12 · Actinomycosis · Streptococci · Staphylococci · Glanders

  • ACTINOMYCOSIS — "lumpy jaw": Actinomyces bovis, a rarefying and proliferative osteomyelitis of the MANDIBLE; pyogranuloma containing "sulphur granules" (club colonies) with the Splendore–Hoeppli reaction
  • ACTINOBACILLOSIS — "wooden tongue": Actinobacillus lignieresii, in SOFT tissue — the tongue becomes hard and protrudes; same club colonies
  • STRANGLES: Streptococcus equi subsp. equi in young horses — fever, nasal discharge and abscessation of the submandibular and retropharyngeal lymph nodes which rupture and discharge thick pus. "Bastard strangles" = metastatic abscesses in other organs. PURPURA HAEMORRHAGICA is the Type III immune-complex sequel — widespread petechiae and severe oedema
  • STAPHYLOCOCCOSIS: Staph. aureus — abscess, gangrenous mastitis, tick pyaemia of lambs, botryomycosis (chronic granuloma with club colonies)
  • GLANDERS ★: Burkholderia mallei, of equines, zoonotic and notifiable in India. Nasal form — ulcers on the septum that heal into a typical stellate (star-shaped) scar. Pulmonary form — miliary grey nodules. Cutaneous form = "FARCY" — corded, thickened lymphatics studded with nodules ("farcy buds") that ulcerate. Diagnosed by the MALLEIN test and complement fixation

14 · Leptospirosis · Listeriosis · Swine erysipelas · Brucellosis

DiseaseAgent & lesions
LEPTOSPIROSISLeptospira interrogans serovars — pomona, hardjo, canicola, icterohaemorrhagiae, grippotyphosa. Spread in URINE, enters through wet skin and mucosa; rodents are the reservoir. Acute in calves — intravascular haemolysis with haemoglobinuria ("REDWATER"), jaundice and fever. Chronic — interstitial nephritis giving the "WHITE-SPOTTED KIDNEY", abortion, and agalactia with flabby udder in cows. In dogs, acute nephritis and hepatitis. Diagnosis: dark-field microscopy of urine, MAT, silver stain of kidney, PCR. Important ZOONOSIS (Weil's disease)
LISTERIOSISListeria monocytogenes — Gram-positive, grows at refrigeration temperature (cold enrichment) and multiplies in poorly fermented SILAGE with pH above about 5.5. Three syndromes: ① "CIRCLING DISEASE" — ascends the trigeminal nerve to give MICROABSCESSES IN THE BRAIN STEM (pons and medulla) with unilateral cranial nerve deficits, drooping ear and lip, circling. ② Abortion in the last trimester with focal hepatic necrosis in the fetus. ③ Septicaemia with miliary hepatic necrosis in monogastrics and neonates. Zoonotic
SWINE ERYSIPELASErysipelothrix rhusiopathiae. Acute — septicaemia with DIAMOND-SHAPED (rhomboid) raised red-purple SKIN LESIONS, pathognomonic. Chronic — vegetative endocarditis of the LEFT AV (mitral) valve and proliferative non-suppurative arthritis. Zoonotic (erysipeloid in fish handlers)
BRUCELLOSIS ★Brucella abortus (cattle), B. melitensis (goats, sheep — the most pathogenic for man), B. suis, B. ovis (rams). Facultative intracellular, survives in macrophages; has a predilection for the placenta and fetal fluids because of ERYTHRITOL.
Lesions: abortion in the LAST TRIMESTER, retained placenta and metritis; necrotic yellow leathery cotyledons with a THICKENED, OEDEMATOUS INTERCOTYLEDONARY MEMBRANE covered in sticky exudate; fetal bronchopneumonia; in bulls orchitis and epididymitis with testicular necrosis and seminal vesiculitis; carpal/knee hygroma in Indian cattle.
Tests: RBPT (screening), STAT, milk ring test, CFT, ELISA; culture of fetal abomasal content is the best sample. Zoonosis — undulant/Malta fever

17 · SUPERFICIAL & DEEP MYCOSES

MycosisAgent & diagnostic feature
RINGWORM
(dermatophytosis)		Trichophyton verrucosum — the usual cause in CATTLE, thick grey-white crusty circular plaques around the eyes and neck. Microsporum canis — dogs and cats, and the one that shows apple-green FLUORESCENCE under WOOD'S (UV) LAMP. T. mentagrophytes, M. gypseum. Lesion: circular expanding alopecia with scaling and a healing centre; hyphae and arthrospores confined to the keratin and hair shaft, folliculitis. Diagnosis: 10–20% KOH mount of hair/scale, Wood's lamp, culture on DTM/Sabouraud. Zoonotic
ASPERGILLOSISAspergillus fumigatus; septate, dichotomously branching hyphae at about 45°. Bovine mycotic abortion ★ — thickened leathery necrotic placenta with raised plaques, and discrete raised "ringworm-like" plaques on the SKIN of the aborted fetus. Guttural pouch mycosis of horses — erodes the internal carotid → fatal epistaxis. Brooder pneumonia in chicks. Angioinvasive with infarction. Stain: PAS or GMS
ZYGOMYCOSIS
(mucormycosis)		Rhizopus, Mucor, Absidia. Broad, irregular, NON-SEPTATE (sparsely septate) hyphae branching at wide angles. Strongly angioinvasive → thrombosis and infarction; gastric and abomasal ulcers, lymph node granulomas, mycotic placentitis
HISTOPLASMOSISHistoplasma capsulatum — small intracellular yeasts inside macrophages; granulomatous disease of the intestine, liver, spleen and lung of dogs. H. farciminosum causes EPIZOOTIC LYMPHANGITIS of horses — corded lymphatics with ulcerating nodules (must be told apart from glanders)
CRYPTOCOCCOSISCryptococcus neoformans, associated with pigeon droppings. Yeast with a huge mucoid CAPSULE that produces a gelatinous, "soap-bubble" appearance. Nasal granuloma of the CAT ("Roman nose"), meningitis. Capsule shown by INDIA INK negative staining and by MUCICARMINE (positive) — a favourite question
RHINOSPORIDIOSIS ★Rhinosporidium seeberi — very common in INDIA (cattle, buffalo, horses, dogs and man), linked to stagnant water and mud. Pink, polypoid, friable nasal mass studded with white pinhead specks, which are the SPORANGIA packed with endospores. Bleeds easily, recurs after removal

18 · MYCOTOXICOSES ★★★

MycotoxicosisFungus, toxin, target
AFLATOXICOSISAspergillus flavus and A. parasiticus → aflatoxin B1, the most toxic. On groundnut cake, maize and cottonseed stored damp — the classic Indian feed problem. Target = LIVER; it is both hepatotoxic and HEPATOCARCINOGENIC. Acute: centrilobular necrosis and haemorrhage, icterus, bleeding (↓ clotting factors). Chronic: firm pale fibrotic liver with marked BILE DUCT (biliary) HYPERPLASIA, MEGALOCYTOSIS of hepatocytes, and eventually hepatocellular carcinoma. Ducklings are the most sensitive species; also immunosuppression and poor growth. Aflatoxin M1 is excreted in milk
OCHRATOXICOSISAspergillus ochraceus, Penicillium verrucosum → ochratoxin A. Target = KIDNEY (nephrotoxic). Pale, enlarged, mottled kidneys with tubular degeneration and interstitial fibrosis — "mycotoxic porcine nephropathy"; polyuria, poor growth. Immunosuppressive
TRICHOTHECOSISFusarium species → T-2 toxin, deoxynivalenol (vomitoxin), diacetoxyscirpenol. Trichothecenes are protein-synthesis inhibitors and are RADIOMIMETIC: oral and gastrointestinal necrosis and ulceration, feed refusal and vomiting, plus necrosis of bone marrow and lymphoid tissue → pancytopenia and haemorrhage. Also contact dermatitis of the muzzle
DEGNALA DISEASE ★An Indian condition of buffaloes and cattle in the northern states, linked to feeding mouldy PADDY (rice) STRAW and its Fusarium toxins. Not febrile; response is reported to selenium plus vitamin E and change of fodder

23 · TOXICITIES ★★★

PoisonThe lesion / clue that names it
ARSENICInorganic: severe haemorrhagic gastroenteritis, profuse "RICE-WATER" diarrhoea, bright red velvety abomasal/gastric mucosa, garlicky odour of the ingesta, fatty liver and tubular nephrosis. Organic phenylarsonic feed additives in pigs → peripheral nerve demyelination, blindness and ataxia
COPPER (chronic)SHEEP are highly susceptible; copper accumulates in the liver, then a stressor triggers a sudden HAEMOLYTIC CRISIS — jaundice, haemoglobinuria, enlarged friable yellow-orange liver and dark "GUN-METAL" KIDNEYS. Confirm by liver and kidney copper assay
LEADCommonest in cattle and calves — discarded batteries, paint, used engine oil. Nervous disease: blindness, head-pressing, bellowing, convulsions, champing of jaws; also anaemia. Blood smear: BASOPHILIC STIPPLING with nucleated red cells. Acid-fast intranuclear inclusions in renal tubular epithelium. Confirm on blood, liver and kidney lead
MERCURYOrganic (mercury-treated seed grain) → neurological — ataxia, blindness, laminar cortical necrosis, peripheral neuropathy. Inorganic → corrosive gastroenteritis + severe renal tubular necrosis
CADMIUMNephrotoxic (proximal tubule), testicular degeneration and necrosis, anaemia, poor growth, interferes with zinc and copper
STRYCHNINEBlocks the inhibitory transmitter GLYCINE in the spinal cord → violent TETANIC convulsions with extensor rigidity, "sawhorse" stance and risus sardonicus, TRIGGERED BY NOISE, LIGHT OR TOUCH; the animal stays conscious; death from respiratory paralysis; rigor mortis sets in very quickly. Essentially no gross lesions — analyse stomach content
NITRATE / NITRITE ★Nitrate in oat hay, sorghum, fertiliser and water is reduced to NITRITE in the rumen; nitrite converts haemoglobin to METHAEMOGLOBIN, which cannot carry oxygen. Blood and tissues are CHOCOLATE-BROWN, mucosae are muddy-brown/cyanotic, dyspnoea, rapid death; also abortion. Antidote: METHYLENE BLUE (and ascorbic acid)
HYDROCYANIC ACID ★Cyanogenic glycosides in young, wilted, drought-stressed or frosted SORGHUM/Johnson grass and cassava. Cyanide blocks cytochrome oxidase, so tissues cannot use oxygen. Blood stays BRIGHT CHERRY-RED, mucosae bright red, bitter-almond odour of the rumen contents, death in minutes. Antidote: sodium nitrite followed by sodium thiosulphate
FLUORIDEDental fluorosis — mottled, chalky, pitted, brown-stained, rapidly worn teeth, but ONLY those teeth that were developing during exposure; exostoses (bony outgrowths) on the mandible and metatarsals, stiffness and lameness. Sources: industrial fallout and high-fluoride bore-well water — endemic in parts of India
SELENIUM (excess)"ALKALI DISEASE" (chronic) — loss of hair from the mane and tail, and deformed, sloughing hooves with a circular break at the coronet. "BLIND STAGGERS" (subacute) — wandering, blindness, paralysis. From selenium-accumulating plants on seleniferous soil (Punjab/Haryana belt)
OXALATESIn Oxalis, Setaria, Halogeton, amaranthus, sugar beet tops. Bind calcium → acute hypocalcaemia and tetany, and calcium oxalate crystals in the renal tubules → NEPHROSIS and renal failure. Chronic low intake with grass leads to bone changes
ORGANOPHOSPHATE / CARBAMATEInhibit acetylcholinesterase → acetylcholine accumulates. Muscarinic "SLUD" — Salivation, Lacrimation, Urination, Defecation, plus miosis, bradycardia, bronchoconstriction and pulmonary oedema; nicotinic — muscle tremor then paralysis; CNS — convulsions. Diagnosis: low blood cholinesterase. Antidote: ATROPINE; add 2-PAM (pralidoxime) for organophosphates but NOT for carbamates
ORGANOCHLORINEDDT, BHC/lindane, endosulfan — CNS STIMULATION: hyperexcitability, muscle tremors beginning at the head, exaggerated response to stimuli, convulsions. Highly lipophilic and persistent — residues in fat and milk. No specific antidote
