3 · FORESTOMACHS — the bovine set

  • Ruminal tympany (bloat) — Primary/frothy: legume & lush green pasture, stable foam. Secondary/free-gas: eructation blocked (oesophageal obstruction, vagal indigestion).
  • Proof it was antemortem ★: the BLOAT LINE — a sharp line in the cervical oesophagus, congested below the thoracic inlet and pale above, plus pale, bloodless head and forelimbs and a distended abdomen.
  • Rumenitis–liver abscess complex — grain overload → lactic acidosis → ruminal ulcer → portal spread of Fusobacterium necrophorum → liver abscess → may rupture into the caudal vena cava.
  • Traumatic reticuloperitonitis ("hardware disease") — wire through the reticulum → localised peritonitis; forward → TRAUMATIC PERICARDITIS: thick foul "washing-water" pus and fibrin, the classic bovine heart.
  • Parakeratosis of the rumen — hard concentrate diet; dark, clumped, hardened papillae.

5 · LIVER & PANCREAS

  • Hepatitis — necrotising (ICH, salmonellosis), suppurative (abscess), chronic (cirrhosis)
  • CIRRHOSIS — diffuse fibrosis + regenerative nodules + architectural distortion; hard, shrunken, nodular liver. Causes: aflatoxin, pyrrolizidine alkaloids (Crotalaria, Senecio), copper, chronic fasciolosis, chronic passive congestion (cardiac cirrhosis)
  • "Pipe-stem liver" — thick fibrotic, calcified bile ducts of chronic fasciolosis (cattle); black pigment = parasitic haematin
  • Telangiectasis — dark blood-filled spaces in cattle liver, incidental
  • "Sawdust liver" — pale foci of focal necrosis in feedlot cattle
  • Hepatosis dietetica — pig, vitamin E / selenium deficiency; mosaic pattern of lobular necrosis
  • Nutmeg liver — chronic passive congestion (right heart failure)
  • Pancreas — acute necrotising pancreatitis with chalky-white fat necrosis (dog); pancreatic atrophy → EPI, steatorrhoea
Icterus in India Yellow carcass + dark urine + splenomegaly in a crossbred cow in tick season = babesiosis/theileriosis, not liver disease.

  • Atelectasis — airless collapsed lung; congenital/neonatal (never breathed, sinks in water) vs acquired (compression / obstruction, absorption)
  • DOCIMASIA (flotation) test ★: lung that has breathed FLOATS; unbreathed fetal lung SINKS — used in veterolegal stillbirth cases
  • Emphysema — alveolar (destruction of septa) and interstitial (air in interlobular septa; classic in cattle, may track to the back as subcutaneous emphysema)
  • Atrophic rhinitis of pigs — toxigenic Pasteurella multocida type D + Bordetella bronchiseptica → turbinate atrophy, snout deviation
  • Verminous pneumonia — Dictyocaulus, cranioventral wedge-shaped grey firm areas
  • Aspiration pneumonia — right cranial/middle lobes, foul, gangrenous; drenching accident is the classic cause in India

10 · HEART — pericardium, myocardium, endocardium

LayerLesion and the disease it names
PericardiumFibrinous pericarditis = "bread-and-butter" heart. Traumatic pericarditis of cattle — foul "washing-water" pus + wire. Hydropericardium — mulberry heart disease of pigs (vit E/Se) and hydropericardium syndrome of broilers
Myocardium"TIGER HEART" ★ — pale yellow-white streaks alternating with red on the papillary muscles: FMD in calves (also white muscle disease). Myocarditis in blackleg, trypanosomosis, CSF
EndocardiumVegetative valvular endocarditis — cauliflower-like friable masses. Cattle: right AV (tricuspid); pig: left AV (mitral) with Erysipelothrix and Streptococcus. Source of embolic nephritis and pneumonia
Hypertrophy type CONCENTRIC — pressure overload, thick wall, small lumen (aortic/pulmonic stenosis). ECCENTRIC — volume overload, dilated chamber, wall relatively thin (valvular insufficiency). Cor pulmonale = right heart hypertrophy from lung disease; brisket disease = high-altitude cor pulmonale of cattle.

11 · BLOOD VESSELS

  • Arteriosclerosis — generic hardening; atherosclerosis (lipid plaque) is rare in animals but occurs in hypothyroid dogs and pigs
  • Verminous aneurysm ★ — Strongylus vulgaris in the cranial mesenteric artery of the horse → thrombosis → intestinal infarction and colic
  • Arteritis — equine viral arteritis, malignant catarrhal fever, immune-complex (Type III) vasculitis
  • Lymphangitis — epizootic lymphangitis (Histoplasma farciminosum) and glanders (farcy) in horses; corded lymphatics in Johne's

13 · Bone marrow & white cells

  • Aplastic anaemia / pancytopenia — bracken fern (ptaquiloside), radiation, chloramphenicol, oestrogen toxicity in dogs, trichothecene mycotoxins
  • Myelophthisis — marrow crowded out by tumour
  • Leukaemia — neoplastic cells in blood; lymphoma/lymphosarcoma = solid tumour of lymphoid tissue, may be aleukaemic
  • Polycythaemia — relative (dehydration, the common one) vs absolute (primary or hypoxia-driven)
  • Leucopenia — viral (CSF, BVD, IBD, panleukopenia, RD), overwhelming sepsis, endotoxaemia, radiation

Anthrax rule ★★★ A ruminant found dead with tarry unclotted blood from body orifices, rigor mortis absent or incomplete, and a huge soft spleen — DO NOT OPEN THE CARCASS. 16 · LOWER URINARY TRACT

  • Urolithiasis — struvite (Mg-ammonium-phosphate; concentrate-fed male sheep, goats and bullocks) · silica (range cattle) · oxalate · urate (Dalmatian). Obstruction at the sigmoid flexure / urethral process → "water belly" (urethral rupture, urine in subcutis) or bladder rupture and uroperitoneum
  • Cystitis — ascending, commoner in females
  • ENZOOTIC HAEMATURIA ★ — chronic bracken fern feeding → bladder haemangiomas and carcinomas, persistent haematuria; often with bovine papillomavirus co-factor

17 · FEMALE GENITALIA & PLACENTA

  • Endometritis / metritis — post-partum, retained placenta, dystocia; Trueperella (Arcanobacterium) pyogenes, E. coli
  • PYOMETRA — cystic endometrial hyperplasia–pyometra complex of the bitch, progesterone-driven, E. coli; closed cervix is the dangerous form (endotoxaemia, ↑ risk of rupture)
  • BRUCELLA placentitis ★ — necrotic, yellow, leathery cotyledons + thickened intercotyledonary membrane with a sticky exudate; fetal stomach content is the best sample
  • Mummification (fetus dries, no bacteria, closed cervix) vs maceration (bacteria enter, soft tissues liquefy, bones remain)
  • Cystic ovarian disease — follicular cyst (nymphomania) vs luteal cyst (anoestrus)
  • Hydrallantois / hydramnios, uterine torsion, prolapse
Abortion causes to name Bacterial: brucellosis, leptospirosis, listeriosis, campylobacteriosis, salmonellosis, Chlamydia. Viral: IBR, BVD, EHV-1 (rhinopneumonitis), bluetongue, PPR. Fungal: Aspergillus — raised leathery plaques on the placenta and ringworm-like skin lesions on the fetus. Protozoal: Neospora, Toxoplasma (sheep — white necrotic foci on the cotyledons, "strawberry jam"). Toxic/plant: nitrate, locoweed.

22 · EAR & EYE

  • Otitis externa — Otodectes ear mite, Malassezia, bacteria; long pendulous ears predispose
  • Aural haematoma — blood between cartilage and perichondrium, from head shaking
  • Otitis media/interna — head tilt, extension of pharyngeal infection through the Eustachian tube
  • IBK "pinkeye" ★ — Moraxella bovis, fly-spread; keratoconjunctivitis, corneal ulcer → opacity, possible perforation
  • "BLUE EYE" ★ — anterior uveitis with corneal oedema after infectious canine hepatitis (CAV-1) or its vaccine; a Type III immune-complex reaction
  • Equine recurrent uveitis ("moon blindness") — immune-mediated, associated with Leptospira; leads to cataract, synechia and phthisis bulbi
  • Squamous cell carcinoma of the eye — Hereford and other unpigmented-eyelid cattle, sunlight; commonest bovine eye tumour
  • Cataract — lens opacity (diabetes, age, trauma) · Glaucoma — ↑ intraocular pressure, buphthalmos · Exophthalmos vs enophthalmos
